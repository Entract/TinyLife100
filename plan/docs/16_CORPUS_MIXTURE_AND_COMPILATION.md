# 16 — Corpus Mixture and Compilation

## 1. Purpose

TL100 v2 separates three things that must not be conflated:

1. **Canonical life structure** — what happened/was encountered and in what order.
2. **Available content pool** — Life-native prose, source segments, artifacts, etc.
3. **Training export** — the exact token sequence and sampling schedule a model sees.

This separation lets us test mixture and ordering cleanly.

---

## 2. Material classes

Every training-text unit has a material class.

### `LIFE_NATIVE`

Narrator-authored first-person prose:

- scenes;
- project work;
- reflections;
- explanations;
- debugging;
- planning;
- postmortems;
- notes written by Alex;
- conversations represented inside Alex's narrative.

### `HUMAN_READING`

Human-authored external works deliberately encountered in the Life:

- textbook sections;
- books;
- manuals;
- papers;
- essays;
- literature;
- documentation;
- other approved written sources.

### `TECHNICAL_ARTIFACT`

Artifacts where format itself matters:

- code;
- config files;
- logs;
- data tables;
- specifications;
- terminal output;
- procedures;
- schematics represented textually;
- project documents.

Artifacts may be human-authored, generated, or narrator-authored; origin is stored separately from material class.

### `OTHER_DIALOGUE_OR_DOCUMENT`

Optional class for standalone communications/documents that are intentionally included in native form rather than narrated.

Avoid proliferating classes unless an experiment needs them.

---

## 3. Origin is orthogonal to material class

A material class is not the same as authorship.

Record an origin field such as:

- `human_original`
- `human_public_domain`
- `human_open_licensed`
- `model_generated`
- `model_rephrased_from_source`
- `narrator_generated`
- `mixed_or_edited`

This lets TL100 distinguish:

> first-person Life prose generated by a frontier model

from:

> a human textbook chapter

from:

> a generated technical artifact

without pretending these are interchangeable “synthetic” or “natural” buckets.

---

## 4. No fixed mixture ratio

Do **not** lock TL100 to “30% synthetic / 70% natural” or any other ratio based on unrelated pretraining studies.

Those studies are useful evidence that mixture method matters, not a direct prescription for this corpus.

Instead define:

- a **planning envelope** for capacity/source acquisition;
- multiple **experimental mixtures**;
- a data-driven decision for the eventual large run.

### Provisional planning envelope — not a target

For a 250M-token mature compilation, infrastructure should be capable of supporting approximately:

- 30–70% `LIFE_NATIVE`;
- 20–60% `HUMAN_READING`;
- 5–20% `TECHNICAL_ARTIFACT` / other structured material.

These ranges intentionally overlap and are not meant to sum independently. They define what the system must be able to compile, not the final blend.

---

## 5. Initial mixture experiments

At 5M–25M tokens, compare at least:

| Export | LIFE_NATIVE | HUMAN_READING | ARTIFACT/OTHER | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `pure_life` | 100% | 0% | 0% | baseline for synthetic-life hypothesis |
| `life_heavy` | 70% | 20% | 10% | preserve strong narrator distribution |
| `balanced` | 50% | 35% | 15% | mixed developmental corpus |
| `reading_heavy` | 30% | 55% | 15% | test human-text breadth |

Exact values can change after pilot evidence. The important point is to test meaningful separation.

For matched comparisons, hold total token exposure, tokenizer, architecture, optimizer, seeds where practical, and source subset constant.

---

## 6. Canonical stream versus compiled stream

The canonical timeline can contain:

```text
stream_000001  LIFE_EPISODE      -> ep_00000001
stream_000002  LIFE_EPISODE      -> ep_00000002
stream_000003  READING_START     -> read_00000001
stream_000004  SOURCE_SEGMENT    -> source_0012/seg_03
stream_000005  READING_END       -> read_00000001
stream_000006  LIFE_EPISODE      -> ep_00000003
stream_000007  TECH_ARTIFACT     -> artifact_000004
...
```

The compiler decides which stream items contribute text to a given training export.

Metadata-only boundaries may influence ordering without adding literal tokens.

---

## 7. Compilation views

### A. `life_ordered`

Preserve canonical life order. Readings appear where encountered.

This is the flagship TL100 condition.

### B. `life_local_shuffle`

Preserve broad developmental eras/blocks but shuffle within configured windows.

Tests whether micro-order matters while retaining curriculum phase.

### C. `global_shuffle`

Use the same selected material but randomize passages/episodes globally.

Tests chronology/contextual order.

### D. `decontextualized`

Retain source material and matched Life content, but remove or neutralize reading-event bridges/callbacks as far as experimentally possible.

Tests whether source contextualization adds value beyond content.

### E. `source_flat`

Human readings are grouped or sampled independently from Life chronology.

### F. `life_only`

Exclude explicit external readings.

### G. `source_only_matched`

Selected human reading/artifact pool without Life-native material, where useful as a control.

### H. `curriculum_replay`

Designed order plus controlled replay of older material.

---

## 8. What “same tokens” means

Matched experiments should distinguish:

- **same unique text units**;
- **same tokenized text**;
- **same number of token exposures**;
- **same order** or changed order;
- **same separator/boundary policy**;
- **same batch construction** where possible.

A claim such as “chronology helped” is weak if the chronological model actually saw more repetitions of earlier concepts.

Every export manifest must make these differences explicit.

---

## 9. Context bridges

A reading encounter can create several types of Life-native bridge:

1. **pre-reading trigger** — why the source is sought;
2. **knowledge-state framing** — what is confusing beforehand;
3. **during-reading notes/questions**;
4. **immediate response**;
5. **application**;
6. **later callback**;
7. **semanticized transfer** — later behavior changes without explicit recollection.

The compiler should tag these bridge episodes so decontextualization experiments can remove matched subsets systematically rather than manually.

---

## 10. Preserve coherent source units

Default compilation should preserve source order at the level deliberately selected for reading.

Examples:

- chapter order;
- section order;
- contiguous manual procedure;
- code file/module order where meaningful.

Experimental fragmentation can be created as an ablation, but should never overwrite canonical source segmentation.

---

## 11. Source repetition and rereading

Rereading is a Life event, not hidden duplicate data.

If Alex rereads a chapter:

- the canonical stream can reference the same immutable source segment again;
- the export manifest counts the second exposure;
- the reading event records why it recurs;
- the unique-token count does not count the source text twice;
- training-token exposure does.

This gives us explicit replay rather than accidental duplication.

---

## 12. Mixture by era

The optimum mixture may change through the Life.

A plausible pattern to test:

- early eras: high Life-native experiential prose, modest short readings;
- mid technical eras: increasing manuals/textbooks/docs;
- later ML/LLM eras: more technical papers, code, documentation, specifications;
- throughout: bounded literature/ordinary reading for linguistic/human breadth.

Do not enforce a flat 50/35/15 mixture at every era.

The model's “library diet” should itself evolve.

---

## 13. Mixture metrics

Every compiled release should report:

- tokens by material class;
- tokens by origin;
- unique source authors/works;
- source concentration (largest author/work shares);
- narrator-prose share;
- explicit reading share;
- artifact share;
- repeated-exposure share;
- average source-unit length;
- average bridge-token ratio around readings;
- era distribution;
- concept distribution;
- lexical duplication/similarity;
- provenance completeness.

---

## 14. Mature-corpus planning

The 250M-token v4 target should be understood as a **compiled corpus budget**, not a requirement to author 250M synthetic tokens.

Possible eventual outcomes include:

```text
Outcome A
150M Life-native + 75M readings + 25M artifacts

Outcome B
100M Life-native + 125M readings + 25M artifacts

Outcome C
75M Life-native + 150M readings + 25M artifacts
```

These are examples, not recommendations.

The pilot and 5M/25M experiments decide which direction is worth scaling.

---

## 15. Central scientific question introduced by v2

The project now supports a stronger question than “is synthetic Life prose efficient?”

> **Does embedding heterogeneous human knowledge within one coherent developmental experience change what a small model learns from the same information?**

That is the principal reason the canonical stream, source registry, and compilation layer must remain separate.
